A note from Elena

I experienced my first migraine as a teenager. At its worst, I lived with more than twenty migraine days a month. For years, I searched for answers that conventional approaches couldn't fully provide.

That search led me to neuroscience and, eventually, to a question that would shape my life's work: what if migraine was not simply a neurological disorder, but also a disorder of brain energy metabolism?

I began investigating the relationship between mitochondrial function, energy metabolism and migraine, ultimately developing a framework that I call “metabolic migraine.”

What began as a personal journey became a scientific mission, one that continues to guide my research, the innovations we develop at KetoSwiss, and my broader vision for brain health.

Pioneering the concept of metabolic migraine

Dr. Gross coined the term “metabolic migraine” to describe a proposed subgroup of migraine associated with metabolic abnormalities. Her research explores how mitochondrial function, oxidative stress and impaired energy metabolism may contribute to migraine susceptibility.

Through peer-reviewed publications, clinical biomarker research and a randomized clinical trial, she has contributed to a growing scientific understanding of the relationship between metabolism and neurological health.

Her work now extends beyond migraine, investigating the potential of metabolic and nutritional approaches to support brain health more broadly, with a focus on mental health and aging.

“Metabolic migraine” is a research-based concept and proposed subgroup, not an established clinical diagnosis.

First author · Clinical biomarker study

Scientific ReportsTrial analysis · 2023

Defining metabolic migraine with a distinct subgroup of patients with suboptimal inflammatory and metabolic markers

First author · Exploratory clinical trial analysis

Building on a randomized, placebo-controlled trial, Dr. Gross investigated the concept of “metabolic migraine,” identifying a potential subgroup characterized by distinct metabolic and inflammatory markers.

In this exploratory analysis, 9 of 32 evaluable participants met the response criteria for ketone supplementation, experiencing approximately 5.8 fewer migraine days compared with placebo. The findings identified readily measurable blood biomarkers that could help guide more personalized metabolic interventions in future research.

Post-hoc, hypothesis-generating findings; prospective validation is needed.

About Elena

I became a neuroscientist to understand my own brain.

I grew up with migraine. For years I was told it was just a headache, that my pain wasn't real, or that there was nothing more to be done. I didn't accept that. I went to Oxford to study neuroscience, turned down a PhD place there to pursue an idea they considered too risky, and spent the next fifteen years testing it: that migraine may be, in part, a problem of brain energy.

    The evolutionary paradox

    What if the brain's greatest strength is also its greatest vulnerability?

    Could genetic traits associated with migraine have once conferred an evolutionary advantage?

    Heightened sensory sensitivity, vigilance and neuronal responsiveness may have helped our ancestors detect and respond to environmental threats. Yet these same characteristics could potentially increase the brain's energy demands and vulnerability under modern conditions.

    Her hypotheses

    Five questions, one unresolved puzzle

    From evolutionary theory to testable neuroscience
    1. H1
      Brain excitability

      Could heightened neuronal excitability and sensory sensitivity make the migraine brain both more responsive to its environment and more metabolically vulnerable?

    2. H2
      Mitochondrial function

      Could impaired mitochondrial function and oxidative stress contribute to an imbalance between the brain's energy supply and demand?

    3. H3
      Metabolic migraine and depression

      Can distinct metabolic and inflammatory biomarkers help identify subgroups of people with migraine or depression who may benefit from targeted metabolic interventions?

    4. H4
      Metabolic flexibility

      Can ketone bodies and other alternative energy substrates support neuronal function through mechanisms extending beyond ATP production?

    5. H5
      Evolutionary advantage

      Could genetic traits associated with migraine have once provided a survival advantage through heightened vigilance, sensory awareness and responsiveness to environmental threats?

      Many brain disorders share one feature: the brain runs short of energy.

      12. Do we use online tracking and online advertising techniques?
